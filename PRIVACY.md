## Privacy Policy

In short: there is no sign-up. When you add your first task, prune gives this browser a guest id: a random identifier, with no name, email address or password. Your list is kept in this browser and copied to Supabase under that guest id, so it is backed up and the same in every tab. To offer "Do it for me", the titles of your open tasks are sent to an AI model (through OpenRouter, in the United States), which sorts out which ones it could help with; when you tap "Do it for me", that task, your choices and your other open task titles are sent to it to write the answer. A guest gets three answers; after that, you sign in with Google or an emailed code to keep using it. You can plan a task on the calendar, with how long it takes and a description; these are saved with the task. In a shared space, its calendar shows everyone in it when the others are busy with their own plans, as blurred time only. Five minutes before a planned task starts, everyone in its space gets a notification (if their browser allows them) and, with an email address, an email, saying what it is and when. Delete everything, in the menu, erases your list here and in the cloud. We do not sell your data or use advertising trackers. The details and your rights are below.

This notice explains how personal data is processed when you use prune on the web. It follows the transparency structure of Articles 13 and 14 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It is not a request to consent to all processing, a waiver of your rights, or a guarantee of regulatory compliance.

## 2. What data do we process, and where does it come from?

- Guest id and session: before your first task, prune sends nothing anywhere. When you add it, Supabase Auth creates a guest (an anonymous user) for this browser: a random user id, when it was created and last used, and the access and refresh tokens that keep it signed in. The tokens are kept in this browser's localStorage, where scripts running on this website's origin can access them. To create a guest and protect the service, Supabase processes your IP address and request metadata, including to limit how many guests one address can create. Each time prune opens, it checks the guest id with Supabase. No email address, name or password is collected.
- Your list: the title of each task, whether it is done, when it was created, last changed and finished, and, if you plan it on the calendar, its start, how long it takes and a description (which can include links).
- Calendars and busy time: your Personal calendar also shows the tasks you planned in your shared spaces, with each space's name. A shared space's calendar shows everyone in it when its people are busy with unfinished plans in their other spaces (their Personal space included): only time ranges, merged across everyone, never whose they are, their titles or descriptions. These are worked out from the lists when a calendar is opened and are not stored. The device supplies an IANA timezone with each sync. This is not GPS or precise-location collection.
- "Do it for me" (AI): while you use the list, the titles of your open tasks (at most 20 at a time, each only when it is new or renamed) are sent to the AI model (section 4) so it can say which ones it could help with; the answer (a kind such as "a text to write", or none) is kept in this browser. When you tap "Do it for me" on a task, its title, your other open task titles (at most 30), your browser's time zone and language, and the options you pick are sent to the model, first to write up to three this-or-that questions, then to write the answer. The answer, any changes you ask for and the answers to them are stored in Supabase with your guest id or account, and a copy in this browser, until you discard it, delete the task or choose Delete everything. To apply limits, we count how many answers each guest id or account started (in total, as a guest) and how many requests it made each day.
- Signing in (for "Do it for me"): after a guest's three free answers, you can sign in with Google or with a six-digit code sent to your email address. Supabase Auth then keeps your email address (and, for Google, the name and profile picture Google shares) with a new or existing account, and this browser's list is copied to that account. The browser's guest id is no longer used; its list in the cloud stays until it is cleaned up as an unused guest (section 7).
- Spaces and sharing: a signed-in account (Google or an emailed code) has a Personal space and can be in shared spaces. You can also make a new, empty shared space with the + beside Spaces, under a name you choose. Sharing Personal makes a new shared space with the name you give it (or "Shared list") and one secret link, stored with your account; the tasks that were in Personal move into it and Personal starts empty. Anyone who opens a shared space's link and signs in joins it: their account is recorded as a member, with the time they joined, and their own Personal space is not shared. Everyone in a shared space can see, add, change, tick and delete its tasks, rename it and pass the link on. Everyone in a shared space sees who else is in it: each person's email address and, when their sign-in provider gave one (Google does), their name, and who made the space. A member who leaves loses access; the space's creator can delete it, which erases its tasks for everyone. While a shared space is open, the browser keeps a connection to Supabase Realtime, signed in as your account, so that each change made by anyone in it reaches everyone at once: the message only says which space changed, never what changed, and it is not stored.
- Accounts from before guests: if you signed in with Google or an emailed code before 22 November 2026, that account keeps working in browsers where it is still signed in, and its list syncs as above. It keeps the email address (and, for Google, the name and profile picture Google shared) stored with it. prune was a day planner then. Its data (the tasks such accounts planned, with their days, estimates, status, timer timestamps and recorded focus time; written stop reasons; saved weekly AI check-ins; a dated history of task changes) is no longer shown, analysed, sent anywhere or added to, and is deleted from the database when the day planner is retired from it. Before that, each such account's unfinished planner tasks for that day and later are copied into its list, once; the focus time it chose is kept for its come-back email.
- Connection error reports: when the website cannot reach Supabase Auth to create or check this browser's guest id, it sends a short report to this website's own domain naming the step that failed and whether the request timed out, failed or the browser was offline. The website host adds your approximate country (from your IP address), browser family and whether you are on a phone or computer, then stores the report in Supabase. Your IP address, full browser details and guest id are not stored with the report.
- Local app data: an unencrypted copy of your list, the changes not yet copied to the cloud, and which account the list last synced with, which tasks "Do it for me" can help with and copies of its answers, in this browser's localStorage, plus the combined terms/privacy version and the time you accepted it. Older versions of prune stored other planner data in this browser; prune removes it the next time you open it.
- Reminder emails (accounts with an email address: from before guests, or signed in for "Do it for me"): an account with an email address may get a come-back email when it has not used prune for 7 days, at most once a week and at most four in a row; it contains no task content. To do this we store, with that account, its last reported IANA timezone, the last time (rounded to within an hour) a browser signed in as it contacted prune, its email choices, the time and count of come-back emails and a random unsubscribe token. The emails contain no tracking pixels added by prune. Guests get no emails, and nothing is stored about them for reminders.
- Browser notifications: when you open prune, your browser asks whether prune may show notifications. If you allow them, once this browser has a guest id or account (after your first task) it creates a push subscription (an address at the browser vendor's push service and two encryption keys) and prune stores it with your guest id or account, together with your browser's IANA time zone, the last time this browser opened prune and when it was last sent a notification. Each time prune opens, it updates that time. With it we send at most one come-back notification a day, around 10:00 in your time zone, on days you have not opened prune yet, and stop after 30 days without a visit. Each daily notification is a short line about how you might be feeling (such as "Feeling productive?") with no task content, encrypted on our server for that browser alone (task-start alerts, below, are encrypted the same way). Accounts with an email address also get the come-back reminder there. Blocking notifications for this site in the browser, Delete everything, deleting the account, the browser removing its subscription, or five failed deliveries in a row deletes the stored address.
- Task-start alerts: 5 minutes before each unfinished task planned in one of your spaces starts (a shared space's tasks included), we send a notification to every browser that allowed them, with the task's title, its time and, for a shared space, the space's name; and, if your account has an email address, an email with the same and the task's description. The email has its own link to stop these emails; the notifications stop when you block them for this site. To send each alert once, we record which person was alerted for which task and start time, for two days. Your time zone (as described for reminder emails and notifications) is used to write the time.
- Support data: your message and any attachments or diagnostics you choose to send when contacting us. Do not send sensitive task content unless necessary to resolve your request.
- Operational metadata: hosting and authentication providers may process IP addresses, request timestamps, technical request/device metadata and security logs. These are distinct from your list.

We receive data from you, your interactions with prune, your device's selected timezone, and our authentication and infrastructure providers. We do not obtain data from data brokers.

Avoid entering special-category data (for example health, political or religious information), criminal-offence information, secrets, or other people's personal data into tasks. Only enter information you are entitled to use.

## 4. AI

"Do it for me" uses DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, an openly published model made by DeepSeek, through OpenRouter (a US service that passes each request to a company running the model), as described in section 2: task titles are sent to sort which tasks it can help with, and a task you choose (with your picks, your other open task titles, time zone and language) is sent to write the answer. The model does not search the web. Requests are routed only to providers outside China that, by OpenRouter's settings, do not store prompts to train models; they never go to DeepSeek's or Moonshot's own services. Nothing is sent before you add a task, and no answer is written unless you tap "Do it for me". Answers are generated automatically and can be wrong; they are not decisions about you and have no legal or similarly significant effect. The operator must confirm OpenRouter's and the serving providers' retention periods, data-processing terms and transfer safeguards before public release.

The day planner's automatic weekly AI check-ins and daily task suggestions, which sent selected task activity of signed-in accounts to OpenAI, ended on 22 November 2026: nothing has been sent to OpenAI since. Check-ins saved before then are no longer shown and are deleted with the day planner's other data (section 2). Data already sent to OpenAI was subject to OpenAI's API terms and retention at the time.

## 7. How long is data kept?

- Your list and guest id: until you choose Delete everything, or a justified earlier administrative deletion. Guest ids that have not been used for 12 months may be removed together with their lists; the operator must set up that clean-up before public release (it is not configured yet). The list keeps at most 1,000 tasks: when it is full, the oldest done task is removed from the list to make room for a new one. A deleted task that was not done is removed from the database at once. A done task that is deleted (one at a time, with Clear done, or to make room) leaves your list but is kept in the database, with its title, its space and when it was created, finished and deleted, so we can count how many tasks prune has been used for; it is never shown again. When its space is deleted (by its creator, by Delete everything or with the account), its title and space are erased and only those times remain, as an anonymous count.
- Shared spaces: a space, its name, link and tasks until its creator deletes it, chooses Delete everything or their account is deleted; a membership until the member leaves, the space is deleted, or the member's account is deleted.
- "Do it for me" answers: until you discard the answer, delete its task or choose Delete everything. The usage counts used for its limits: as long as the guest id or account exists.
- Accounts from before guests, and accounts signed in for "Do it for me": until the account is deleted (see section 8). Their earlier planner data: until the day planner is retired from the database (section 2).
- Local list copy: stays in this browser until you choose Delete everything or clear its site storage. Clearing site storage permanently loses the list on that browser, and with it the guest id: a guest's copy in the cloud can no longer be reached from a new guest.
- Local terms acceptance: the current document version and acceptance time remain in this browser profile's localStorage until replaced by a later acceptance or site storage is cleared. This is a device-local record, not an account-wide acceptance register.
- Session credentials: kept in this browser until Delete everything or cleared site storage. Server revocation is best-effort during network failures; an issued access token can remain valid until expiry.
- Connection error reports are removed after 90 days. A browser's push subscription, its time zone and visit times: until notifications are blocked or unsubscribed, Delete everything, the account is deleted, or five deliveries in a row fail. Records of task-start alerts sent: two days. Reminder email settings and send records are kept with their account until it is deleted; copies of sent emails remain in the recipient's mailbox and, for a provider-defined period, in the email provider's logs.
- Deletion from the active database does not immediately erase provider backups. Restoring a backup must not permanently undo an erasure request.
- PostHog analytics data and recordings: for the retention period configured in the PostHog project, which the operator must confirm before public release. Withdrawing consent stops future collection; ask us to erase data already collected.

We may retain only the data necessary for a specific legal obligation or legal claim for the applicable period, restricting its use accordingly. Data that is genuinely anonymised and no longer reasonably identifies anyone may be retained separately. Pseudonymous identifiers alone, such as a guest id, do not make data anonymous.

## 9. Security and device privacy

The app uses HTTPS for production cloud connections, Supabase-managed browser sessions, and per-account database access controls: a browser can reach a list only through a checked request for its own guest id or account. These measures reduce risk but do not make any service completely secure. The guest id's tokens and the local list copy are unencrypted site data: anyone with access to this browser profile can open your list. Use a protected device account and disk encryption; do not keep confidential tasks in a shared browser.

Our application code is designed not to log credentials or task text in error messages. Infrastructure logging, administrator access, backup controls and incident response must also be configured appropriately by the operator. If a personal-data breach occurs, the operator must assess and meet any applicable authority and affected-person notification duties.
